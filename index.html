The problem

Cheap to catch early. Expensive to catch late.

  • Screening is rare and mostly manual.
  • Results are subjective and rarely recorded over time.
  • Families rarely get referred at the right time.

Scope, stated plainly

What SoleShift Kids is — and is not.

What it is

  • Pressure-based screening: foot contact and load distribution.
  • An automatic, readable report.
  • Tracks change over time.
  • Routes families to partners, with consent.

What it is not

  • Not a medical diagnostic device.
  • Doesn't name conditions or treat.
  • Doesn't manufacture insoles — partners do.
  • No data stored without consent.
  • Not a 3D scanner — it doesn't measure foot shape.

Screening ≠ Diagnosis

How it works

From pressure plate to specialist in five steps.

01

Pressure measurement

The child stands on the plate; eight sensors record contact and load.

02

Quality control

Each session is checked for a stable, complete reading before it's kept.

03

Structured report

Load per foot region, in a format a specialist can assess.

04

Specialist assessment

An orthopedic specialist reviews the data and decides — not the software.

05

Families and next steps

Families are informed; where recommended, partners make custom insoles.

What the plate measures — and what it doesn't

Eight force-sensing pads, laid out in the shape of a foot, report how much load each region of the sole places on the plate. That gives the contact pattern and the pressure distribution across heel, midfoot and forefoot, and between left and right. The hardware does not capture the foot's 3D shape, so it does not measure foot length, width or arch height.

The project, in full.

A closer look at the concept, the plan, and the people behind SoleShift Kids.

SoleShift Kids is an engineering and public-health project that supports accessible primary foot-health screening, orthopedic specialist assessment and referral for students and families, including in rural communities. Its platform uses pressure sensors to record how the foot contacts the surface and how pressure is distributed; the data is turned into a structured digital report for specialist assessment.

It is not a medical diagnostic system, and it does not manufacture insoles. Where a specialist recommended custom insoles, they were produced and delivered through partner organizations.

A report can be forwarded to these organizations, always with a separate parental consent — they decide independently what, if anything, to offer the family.

Each stage only starts once the previous one is real, not planned.

  • Screening, not diagnosis — the report suggests a specialist visit, it never names a condition.
  • Parental consent, always — no measurement without it, no sharing without a second, separate consent.
  • Data protected — anonymized by default, no child's face published without written parental consent, deletable on request.
  • Real numbers only — every figure on this site is something that actually happened.
  • Honest about the AI — if it's a rule-based measurement pipeline, that's what we call it.
  • Partners treat, we don't — SoleShift Kids builds the referral infrastructure, not the treatment.
